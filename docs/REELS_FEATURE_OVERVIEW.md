# Swarnix Studio Web — Reels Feature Overview

**Purpose of this document:** a complete, accurate description of what the Reels
feature does today in Swarnix Studio Web, so it can be shared with an outside
reviewer (Claude.ai) and used to answer: *what else could be built here to give
more value to the customer?*

**Product context:** Swarnix Studio Web is a standalone, self-serve web app that
sells AI content-creation tools to **Indian jewellers** (small and mid-size
retail jewellery shops). Users sign in with Google — no approval, no sales call —
get 3 free credits, and top up with Razorpay. There are no subscription tiers:
everything is unlocked and gated purely by a **credit balance**.

The app ships six Studio Suite features: Studio Photo, Metal Swap, AI Model,
Jewellery Design, **Generate Reels**, and Library. This document covers Reels
only.

**Who the customer is (important for evaluating ideas):** the typical user is a
jewellery shop owner or their shop-floor staff member, often working from a
phone, posting to Instagram Reels / WhatsApp Status / Facebook. They are not
video editors. They photograph stock on a counter or in a small light-box. Their
goal is a steady flow of attractive posts that make pieces look premium and
bring walk-ins or WhatsApp enquiries.

---

## 1. What Reels does, in one line

The jeweller gives Swarnix one or more still photos of a jewellery piece and
gets back a finished, downloadable short video (MP4) with AI-generated camera
motion, optional music, and optional burned-in contact text — sized for
Instagram Reels, feed posts, or YouTube.

---

## 2. The two creation modes

The Reel Studio opens on a mode toggle. The two modes are genuinely different
products sharing one render pipeline.

### 2.1 Storyboard mode (the default)

**Idea:** upload **one** photo. AI looks at it, writes a **multi-scene script**,
and the jeweller edits that script before spending anything.

Flow:

1. Jeweller adds **one** photo (device upload, phone camera, or a pick from
   their Studio Library of previously generated images).
2. They set format, length, quality, music, contact text, and an optional extra
   instruction.
3. They press **"Analyse & write script."** This step is **free** — no credits
   are reserved or charged.
4. Google Gemini reads the actual photo server-side and returns:
   - a one-line **analysis** of the piece ("a yellow-gold kundan necklace…"),
   - a **style name** for the overall look,
   - **N scenes**, each with a `title`, a plain-English `prompt` describing the
     shot, and an optional 6-word on-screen `caption`.
5. The jeweller lands on a **storyboard editing screen**: every scene is an
   editable text box, plus its caption. They can rewrite any scene freely, or
   press **"Regenerate script"** to get a fresh set of ideas (also free).
6. They press **Generate Reel**. Each scene is rendered as its own clip, and the
   clips are stitched into one video.

**What the AI is instructed to do** (this is a deliberate, tuned prompt, not a
generic one):

- It first classifies the photo as **model-worn** vs **product-only**.
- If a **model** is present, *every* scene must keep the **same model** wearing
  the **same piece** — no switching to product shots mid-reel. Scene ideas skew
  to: she turns her head so the piece catches the light, her hand rises to frame
  the necklace, slow-motion walk, a glance to camera as stones sparkle, wind in
  her hair/dupatta, a wide-to-close reveal.
- If it is **product-only**, scene ideas skew to: the ring drops into water with
  slow-motion ripples, the piece resting on flowing silk or velvet, golden light
  rays sweeping the stones, drifting petals or light particles, a slow turn on a
  pedestal under a moving spotlight, a hand placing the piece on marble.
- Scenes must be **distinct** — an explicit instruction not to make every scene
  another zoom-in on the jewellery.
- Language must be **simple, plain English** (the customer has to read and edit
  it).

**Per-scene AI first frame — the thing that makes Storyboard mode expensive and
good:** for each scene, the pipeline sends the original photo *plus* that
scene's text to Gemini image generation and gets back a **bespoke photorealistic
still** for that scene — the piece on silk, the piece mid-splash, the model in a
new setting. That generated still becomes the **first frame** for that scene's
video clip. So a single counter photo becomes several genuinely different-looking
cinematic shots, not several crops of the same picture.

Guard rails appended server-side to every scene prompt, which the customer
cannot break:

- **Fidelity guard** — the piece itself must stay exactly as photographed: same
  design, stones, metal colour, shape, proportions. If a person is shown, her
  face and identity stay unchanged. No text, logos, or watermarks generated by
  the model. Backgrounds, lighting, and effects *are* allowed to be creative.
- **Pacing guard** — smooth cinematic slow motion; a gentle push-in, soft arc,
  or slow reveal; no spinning, no full 360° rotation; the jewellery always stays
  in frame and in sharp focus.

**Scene count** is derived from length: under 8s → 1 scene; 8–12s → 2 scenes;
over 12s → 3 scenes. Every clip must be at least 4 seconds (the video model's
minimum), and the count is capped at 6. Durations always sum exactly to the
chosen reel length. If the jeweller's edited scenes carry valid durations that
sum correctly, those are honoured; otherwise the length is split evenly.

### 2.2 Classic mode

**Idea:** the jeweller already has the shots they want. Upload **up to 6 photos
in scene order** and Swarnix animates them, in that order, into one reel.

- Each image gets its own clip. Clip count is
  `min( max(lengthBucket, imageCount), floor(length/4), 6 )`, so every clip stays
  at least 4 seconds. With 6 images you need a 24s reel for all of them to
  appear; with less time, trailing images are dropped rather than producing a
  broken render.
- No AI script, no per-scene generated frames — the jeweller's own photos are the
  first frames. Cheaper and more predictable.
- Best for: a jeweller showing 4 real bangles they actually stock, in order, or
  someone who has already produced good images elsewhere in the Suite.

**The mode split in plain terms:** Storyboard is "make me an advert from this one
photo." Classic is "animate the photos I already chose."

## 3. Settings available in both modes

| Setting | Options | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Format / aspect ratio** | 9:16 (Instagram Reel / WhatsApp Status), 1:1 (Instagram / Facebook post), 16:9 (YouTube / Facebook video) | 9:16 is default |
| **Length** | 4–24 seconds, slider, 1s steps | Default 8s. UI shows a live hint: single- / two- / three-scene reel |
| **Quality** | 480p (SD), 720p (HD, default/recommended), 1080p (Full HD) | Drives price. All three render at the selected resolution |
| **Music** | "No music", a curated library track, or **upload your own** (≤10 MB audio) | Library tracks have a name, a mood tag, and an in-page play/pause preview. Uploaded tracks are trimmed to reel length. Copyright is the user's responsibility, stated in the UI |
| **Contact text overlay** | Up to 80 chars, e.g. "Contact XYZ Jewellers · 98765 43210" | Burned into the video |
| — overlay font | Modern (sans), Classic (serif), Mono | Live preview in the form |
| — overlay colour | White, Gold, Cream, Navy, Black, Red | Live preview in the form |
| — overlay position | "At the end" (last 1–4s, scaled to reel length) or "Whole reel" | |
| **Extra instruction** | Free text, up to 300 chars | e.g. "focus on the diamond, slow elegant motion." Appended to every scene |

**Image sources** in both modes: device file upload, direct **phone camera
capture**, or the **Studio Library** — meaning output from Studio Photo, Metal
Swap, AI Model, or Jewellery Design can be fed straight into a reel without
re-uploading. Images are compressed client-side and hosted on Cloudinary; 5 MB
per-image cap.

## 4. Rendering and delivery

- Rendering is **asynchronous**. On submit the user gets a `job_id` immediately
  and is moved to a progress screen: *"Creating your reel… you can leave this
  screen — it'll be in your Library when it's done."*
- The screen subscribes to **Supabase Realtime** on that job row, so it flips to
  the finished video the moment the render completes — no polling by hand, no
  refresh.
- Typical render time quoted to the user: **1–4 minutes**.
- The finished reel plays inline in an HTML5 player with a **poster frame**
  auto-derived from the video's first frame, plus an **Open / Download** link.
- Failures show a clear message and state that the user was not charged.
- Every reel lands in the user's **Library**, private to them, listed newest
  first, with delete. Row-level security scopes reel rows to the owning user.

**Pipeline under the hood** (n8n + Supabase + Cloudinary):

1. Validate and plan clips server-side (never trusting client values).
2. Create the job row, respond with `job_id`.
3. Per scene (Storyboard only): fetch reference image → Gemini generates the
   bespoke first frame → upload frame to Cloudinary.
4. Per clip: submit to **Seedance 1.5 Pro Fast** (image-to-video) with the
   scene's motion prompt, resolution, ratio, and duration; audio generation off;
   watermark off.
5. Poll all clip tasks in a wait loop until every clip is done or a stop
   condition trips.
6. Hand the ordered clip URLs, music, and overlay spec to a render service that
   stitches, adds the audio track, and burns in the overlay text.
7. Update the job row with the final URL (or the failure), which pushes to the
   user's screen via Realtime.

Per-render cost is tracked internally (Seedance is billed by the second) in both
USD and INR on every job.

## 5. Pricing and billing

Everything is priced in **credits**. Reels are the most expensive feature in the
Suite, and cost scales with **length × quality**:

| Quality | Credits per second |
|---|---|
| 480p (SD) | 0.35 |
| 720p (HD) | 0.75 |
| 1080p (Full HD) | 1.6 |

Cost is `ceil(seconds × rate)`, minimum 1 credit. **Storyboard mode adds +1
credit per scene**, because each scene requires its own AI-generated first frame
(an extra image generation). The exact cost is displayed live in the form and on
the Generate button before the user commits, and it updates as they drag the
length slider or switch quality.

Worked examples: an 8-second reel costs about **3 credits at SD, 6 at HD, 13 at
Full HD**. New users get **10 free credits** — enough for 4 studio images and one 8-second HD reel.

**Two different billing models, deliberately:**

- **Classic reels: reserve-on-submit, refund-on-fail.** Credits are reserved
  atomically at submit so a user cannot queue reels they can't pay for. A
  database trigger refunds server-side if the render later fails; the UI also
  refunds the narrow case where submit throws before a job row exists.
- **Storyboard reels: charge-on-complete.** Nothing is deducted until the render
  actually succeeds — a database trigger charges the server-computed cost on the
  transition to `completed`. A failed storyboard reel is never debited, so there
  is no refund to chase. If the balance is somehow short at completion, the reel
  is still delivered and left uncharged — deliberately in the customer's favour.

Cost is always recomputed server-side from the validated plan; the client's
number is display-only.

## 6. What is deliberately *not* in Reels today

Listing these so the reviewer doesn't propose what already exists, and can judge
whether any absence is a real gap:

- No trimming, splitting, reordering, or re-rendering of individual scenes after
  a reel is generated — regeneration means running the whole reel again.
- No preview of a scene before committing to the full reel; the storyboard is
  text-only at edit time.
- No text/caption burned in **per scene** — the AI writes a `caption` per scene
  and the jeweller can edit it, but the only overlay guaranteed burned into the
  final video is the single contact-text overlay.
- No logo or brand watermark on the reel (Store Branding exists elsewhere in the
  app but is not wired into reels).
- No price, weight, purity, or product-detail overlays.
- No direct publish or share to Instagram / WhatsApp / Facebook from the app —
  the user downloads the MP4 and posts it themselves.
- No scheduling, calendar, or posting cadence tooling.
- No templates or saved presets — every reel is configured from scratch.
- No batch mode — one reel at a time, one piece at a time.
- No voice-over, narration, or text-to-speech; no language selection.
- No festival or occasion awareness inside Reels (Festival Posters is a separate
  feature that does not feed Reels).
- No analytics on what performed well after posting.

## 7. The question for review

Given the above — an Indian jewellery retailer, phone-first, non-technical,
credit-billed, self-serve, who wants a steady flow of premium-looking social
content that drives walk-ins and WhatsApp enquiries:

**What else could be built into Reels to create more value for this customer?**

Useful lenses for the answer:

- Gaps in the *creation* experience (control, speed, confidence before spending).
- Gaps in the *outcome* — what happens after the MP4 exists, and whether the app
  should own more of that journey.
- Ways to raise the perceived quality of the output itself.
- Features that would raise credit consumption in a way the customer is happy to
  pay for, versus features that mainly reduce friction.
- Anything specific to the Indian jewellery retail context — festivals, wedding
  season, regional languages, gold rate movement, bridal vs daily-wear ranges,
  WhatsApp-first distribution — that a generic reel tool would miss.
- Which ideas are cheap to add on top of the existing pipeline versus which need
  genuinely new infrastructure.
